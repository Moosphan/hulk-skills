# Cover Style

Use this reference before writing prompts for daily recipe social-post covers.

## Shared Style

### Style Lock For Corrections

- For a correction or regeneration of an existing work, preserve the source image's established style exactly: canvas ratio, light/dark balance, paper or card material, typography character, illustration accents, section geometry, and information density. Only improve food realism and cross-panel consistency. Do not introduce a new scrapbook, brush-calligraphy, editorial, full-bleed, or dark style during a realism correction.
- Keep the same dish identity across every image and panel. The final plating panel must be a visual match to the work's hero dish image, including vessel, meat cut, pepper variety, cut size, color ratio, garnish, sauce gloss, and arrangement.

- 明亮浅色: warm ivory paper, pale wood tabletop, cream ceramics, soft daylight, light green and terracotta accents.
- 中文手写体: Every visible text element must use one consistent cute, rounded Chinese handwritten typeface, like a warm family recipe notebook or Xiaohongshu food note. This includes the title, section headers, ingredient labels, step titles, step notes, tips, and calorie badge. Keep it soft, lively, simplified Chinese, and clearly legible; do not mix printed, serif, sans-serif, or brush-calligraphy fonts. Keep small labels spacious and readable; do not shrink text into cramped, distorted, or garbled micro-writing.
- Food should be realistic, glossy, appetizing, and home-cooked, with 色香味俱全: balanced appetizing color contrast, visible steam or aroma cues, natural sauce/oil sheen, and textures that feel tender, crisp, juicy, or soft as appropriate.
- Promotional plating covers must show appetizing color-aroma-flavor depth: layered natural color contrast, light steam, sauce cling, oil gloss, moist cut surfaces, crisp/tender cues, and a clean table scene that makes the dish look freshly cooked, fragrant, and publishable.
- Cooked food should show believable cooked texture: visible heat, light steam, sauce or oil sheen, softened vegetables, seared meat edges, tender fish flakes, glossy egg curds, or browned tofu edges as appropriate.
- Rice realism is critical. Cooked white rice, brown rice, or mixed grains should look soft, moist, slightly translucent, naturally varied, and gently clumped by starch. Avoid oversized hard grains, plastic beads, corn-kernel texture, gravel-like particles, dry cereal, or perfectly separated decorative dots.
- Ingredient form must be faithful to the recipe: whole shrimp stays whole with heads/shells when the dish calls for 大虾 or 带头虾; peeled shrimp appears only when the dish calls for 虾仁.
- Cooking-step panels must use plausible real food stages, correct ingredient scale, and the same ingredients as the final dish. Every step must be preparation-state accurate: a panel can show only the ingredient form required at that exact point, never a whole ingredient when the step requires a cut, deboned, separated, cooked, or leftover part.
- Ingredient cards must be a complete visual inventory: every ingredient, seasoning, garnish, marinade component, cooking liquid, and finishing oil named in a visible step must appear as an individual card or in a labeled compound card that names every component.
- Use concrete visual identifiers for easily confused food. Soybean sprouts must appear as separate whole sprouts with slender pale ivory stems and attached round yellow bean heads; in short, they should read as pale ivory stems with small yellow heads, never resembling shredded cheese, noodles, cabbage, julienned potato, or generic strips. Minced garlic should be small, irregular off-white chopped pieces with sharp cut edges. Each ingredient card should show a real food specimen with believable color, moisture, scale, and cut shape.
- Texture artifact control is required for all covers and recipe posters. Key food surfaces must be smooth, polished, and free of jagged speckled artifacts: no saw-tooth edges, pixelated boundaries, gritty speckles, random black or white spots, pitted/diseased-looking skins, repetitive seed-like dots, lumpy plastic blobs, melted wax texture, or over-sharpened crunchy noise. Eggplant should have smooth glossy purple skin and soft creamy flesh; diced meat should have rounded browned edges and juicy coated surfaces; braised beef should show tender fibers; peanuts should be oval, whole, lightly wrinkled, evenly roasted, and spot-free; shiitake should have smooth brown caps and pale gills.
- Thin-sliced pork and sliced meats need cut-specific realism: show varied irregular slices with natural fat-to-lean layers, supple curled edges, lightly translucent rendered fat, and a moist sauce sheen. Do not show dry, shrunken, uniformly cut rectangles, identical tiles, thick cubes, rigid strips, or brittle-looking lean meat.
- Pepper varieties need specimen-level realism in ingredient cards and cooking panels. For `螺丝椒`, show slender irregularly twisted deep-green chilies with longitudinal wrinkles, thin walls and tapered tips; after stir-frying, show glossy folded segments with light blistering. For `小米椒`, show tiny narrow pointed bright-red chilies and thin rings only after cutting. Never substitute smooth generic green peppers, jalapenos, bell peppers, thick generic rings, plastic tubes, or another chili variety.
- Inspect generated covers before delivery. If ingredients look substituted, a panel shows the wrong preparation state, required ingredients are missing, or text uses printed/mixed fonts, revise the prompt and regenerate the affected image.
- Layout should feel clean, warm, airy, and publishable.
- Visible dish names should be standardized dish-style names, not serving-container names. Do not make handwritten cover labels or recipe-poster titles end with `碗`, `盘`, `餐盒`, `便当`, `套餐`, `拼盘`, or `组合` unless the user explicitly requests an established name. Prefer names like `蒜香鸡腿排`, `黑椒牛肉炒彩椒`, or `番茄虾仁滑蛋`.
- 不要使用暗色系: avoid black background, dark navy, dark brown, black-gold poster style, heavy shadows, nightclub lighting, or formal banquet mood.

## 菜品预览摆盘图

Purpose: show how the dish or dishes look on the table as the promotional first image.

Prompt requirements:

- Vertical 3:4.
- For a single-dish work, show one finished dish as the hero plated meal image.
- For a multi-dish work, show all dishes plated together as one meal set.
- Pale wood tabletop, warm ivory linen, matching ceramic bowls or plates.
- Optional rice, chopsticks, spoon, small sauce dish, scallions, or chili as supporting items.
- If rice or grains appear as supporting items, specify cooked-rice realism: soft moist grains, natural starch clumps, correct small scale, not pellets, beads, corn, gravel, or dry cereal.
- Add small legible Chinese handwritten labels near each dish. For single-dish work, use one label.
- The label text must be a proper dish name, not a vessel or meal-format name. Use `蒜香鸡腿排` instead of `蒜香鸡腿排南瓜糙米碗`; use `柠檬香草煎鳕鱼` instead of `柠檬香草鳕鱼盘`.
- No recipe steps or ingredient grid.

## 菜谱封面

Purpose: teach one dish at a glance.

Prompt requirements:

- Vertical 3:4.
- Large handwritten Chinese dish title.
- The title must be a standardized dish name and should not end with container or meal-format words such as `碗`, `盘`, `餐盒`, `便当`, `套餐`, `拼盘`, or `组合`.
- Main finished dish photo.
- 食材准备 section with small dishes/cards and handwritten labels.
- 4-6 compact cooking step panels with short Chinese step titles.
- A short 小贴士 strip at the bottom.
- A clear calories badge or strip, e.g. `约320 kcal/份` or `整盘约520 kcal`.
- Keep all visible text simplified Chinese and readable.
- For each step panel, specify exact ingredient form and stage: raw, marinated, cut, stir-fried, steamed, plated. Avoid fake or generic food shapes.
- For each action, specify the correct utensil, vessel, and immediately visible result. If a recipe uses a by-product such as bones, shells, or trimmings, list it in the ingredients and show only that by-product in the relevant panel. Never substitute an intact whole ingredient for a separated component.
- Finished-dish and plated step panels that include rice or grains must state cooked texture explicitly: moist, soft, lightly glossy, naturally clumped, and correctly scaled.
- Step panels must use consecutive step numbers with no missing or skipped numbers. For example, 7 panels must show labels 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 exactly once.
- Time-sensitive step panels must include a concrete cooking duration in visible Chinese text. Use exact phrases such as `蒸8分钟`, `焖12分钟`, `烤18分钟`, `小火炖45分钟`, `煎3分钟`, or `腌10分钟`; never leave users with vague text such as `蒸熟`, `煮熟`, `烤熟`, `焖一会儿`, or `适量时间`.
- Keep recipe-poster visible text short enough for the card size: compact labels, stable line spacing, no cramped micro-writing, no distorted strokes, no garbled characters. Add or simplify panels instead of forcing long paragraphs into tiny cards.

## Prompt Checklist

Each prompt should state:

- dish name
- standardized visible cover title or label that does not end with a serving-container or meal-format suffix
- serving size if known
- main visual ingredients
- exact ingredient form, especially whole shrimp vs peeled shrimp, bone-in vs boneless, raw vs cooked step stages
- cooked-food texture details, especially realistic cooked rice or grain texture when present
- background and palette
- handwritten Chinese typography
- one consistent cute, rounded Chinese handwritten typeface for every visible text element
- exact cover type: plating preview or recipe poster
- estimated calories for recipe posters
- negative constraints against dark style, random watermark, QR code, logo, extra hands, mixed fonts, printed fonts, unreadable Chinese, fake-looking food, malformed ingredients, ingredient substitutions, inaccurate preparation states, whole-vs-cut contradictions, oversized rice grains, pellet-like rice, plastic bead texture, and dry cereal-like grains
- negative constraints against jagged speckled artifacts, gritty noise, random spots, pitted skins, pixelated ingredient edges, lumpy plastic blobs, melted wax texture, and over-sharpened food surfaces
- negative constraints against weak appetizing presentation, flat dull food color, dry or waxy cooked surfaces, cramped text, distorted handwriting, and garbled micro-writing
- negative constraints against missing step numbers, skipped step numbers, duplicate numbers, or broken step order
- negative constraints against missing cooking durations for steaming, roasting, baking, simmering, braising, boiling, blanching, frying-to-doneness, marinating, resting, or sauce-reducing steps
